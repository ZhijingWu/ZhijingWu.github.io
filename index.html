Execution-Reliability Diagnostics of Language Agent Tree Search

Independent Research Audit / Language Agents

Investigated action-output contracts and evaluator-score extraction in a pinned LATS/WebShop implementation using a bounded real-model execution trace, controlled frozen-prompt comparisons, and offline parser verification.

  • Action interface: 35/45 generated actions were invalid in the recorded baseline prefix. A format instruction changed syntax compliance from 0/15 to 15/15 completions; all were think[...], with no navigation demonstrated.
  • Score interface: complete final scores changed from 0/6 at 100 tokens to 6/6 at 300 tokens. The native parser agreed with only 3/6; the digit 5 in $50 explained all three mismatches.

Scope. One baseline task and selected frozen prompts. No benchmark-wide prevalence estimate, demonstrated task-success improvement, or real mixed-frontier child/value alignment exposure.

Study design, B1–B4 evidence & limitations
B1
In one bounded real-model baseline prefix, 35/45 generated actions were invalid, and 32/32 evaluator outputs lacked complete final scores at the 100-token cap. No successful purchase occurred. A request ceiling interrupted the first rollout before a completed backup.
B2
A command-format instruction changed syntax compliance from 0/15 to 15/15 completions. Each condition used 3 requests with 5 completions per request; the completions are clustered within requests. All 15 compliant outputs were think[...] commands. No navigation or environment episode was demonstrated.
B3
Across three frozen evaluator prompts and two repetitions per prompt/cap, complete final scores changed from 0/6 at 100 tokens to 6/6 at 300 tokens. These were fresh samples, not continuations. The native parser agreed with 3/6 complete scores; all three mismatches came from the digit 5 in $50. No search or environment episode was run in this comparison.
B4
81/81 private CPU checks passed: 64 synthetic cases, 12 recorded-response comparisons and 5 synthesis/boundary checks. The public repository provides only the 64 synthetic parser tests, not the full private real-response suite. Public raw real-model responses are withheld.

The public synthetic tests demonstrate source-level parser behavior; they do not independently reproduce the original real-model observations. Score-extraction agreement does not validate shopping judgments. Neither a new LATS algorithm nor an evaluated search-performance fix is claimed.

This independent audit is not affiliated with or endorsed by the LATS or WebShop authors. Its small selected sample and withheld raw responses limit generalization and public verification.

Communication-Cost Sensitivity in LLM Multi-Agent Protocol Assignment

Diagnostic / Sensitivity Study

Can measured local communication costs change a fixed protocol assignment—and predict workflow latency?

  • 480/480 requests succeeded. An eligible A2A → ANP reversal persisted after excluding Agora and separating forced candidate-set changes.
  • 120 workflows / 480 additional RPCs. Serial latency difference: predicted −0.156 ms, observed +0.828 ms; parallel comparisons were order-sensitive.

Scope. A controlled sensitivity diagnostic and separate within-session calibration; no established GAIA task-quality gain or general protocol superiority.

Full study description — ProtocolBench

Independent Study on ProtocolBench

I audited the released ProtocolBench / AgentProtocols GAIA routing path to ask whether workflow communication demand and measured runtime costs can change a fixed local protocol assignment.

I profiled A2A, ACP, ANP, and Agora over real local request paths with 1–100 KiB payloads; 480/480 measured requests succeeded. Under controlled communication demand, synthetic local preference margins, and released workflow configuration templates, I found an A2A → ANP assignment reversal in the declared sensitivity objective. The eligible reversal persisted in a no-Agora ablation after separating forced candidate-set changes.

In a separate workflow-latency calibration, I measured 120 no-op workflows (an additional 480 RPCs) with serial and fork/join execution. The within-session serial treatment-minus-baseline latency was predicted as −0.156 ms but observed as +0.828 ms; parallel comparisons were sensitive to execution order.

Assignment sensitivity and workflow calibration are distinct results; neither establishes GAIA task-quality gains or general protocol superiority. The linked public artifact documents the assignment diagnostic.

Execution-Semantics Audit of Guided Topology Diffusion

Reproduction / Execution-Semantics Audit

Do generated communication graphs retain their meaning when constructed and executed by the runtime?

  • 603 records. Mean raw-to-executed edge Jaccard increased from 0.152 to 0.863 with a controlled coordinate adapter.

Scope. Improved graph fidelity did not establish downstream task-performance gains.

Full study description — GTD

Multi-Agent Systems Reproduction Study

I reproduced the released GTD pipeline on GSM8K and traced generated communication graphs through diffusion supervision, runtime construction, execution, and final aggregation.

Across 603 records, mean raw-to-executed edge Jaccard was 0.152. A controlled canonical-coordinate adapter increased it to 0.863. Downstream task-performance gains were not established.

Within-Step Corruption Timing for Computer-Use Agents

Independent Extension / Agent Evaluation

How does corruption timing within the perception–action cycle affect computer-use agents?

  • 3 Chrome tasks · 36 primary runs. Stale-timing mean episode lengths increased by +4.00, +1.75 and +1.00 steps.
  • Profile rename: 2/4 successes under stale timing versus 4/4 before observation.

Scope. Small, descriptive comparison with uncontrolled sampling seeds; failed/capped episodes are included, and recovery work is not isolated.

Full study description — AgentHijack

Independent Extension of AgentHijack

I studied pre-observation, post-observation/pre-execution, and post-execution corruption timing across 3 Chrome tasks and 36 primary runs.

Post-observation/pre-execution corruption increased mean episode length relative to pre-observation on all 3/3 retained tasks: +4.00, +1.75, and +1.00 steps. Profile-rename success was 2/4 under stale timing versus 4/4 under pre-observation corruption.

A descriptive comparison with a small task set and uncontrolled sampling seeds. Episode-length summaries include failed/capped episodes and do not isolate recovery work or establish explicit specification violations.

Trust-Metadata Inheritance in Continually Updated LLM Memory

Independent Follow-up / Memory Audit

Should edited memory rules inherit the support accumulated for their previous text?

  • Rank 2 → 15 when support was reset in a shared-state EDIT replay, with all 36 rule texts preserved.
  • 50 paired WebShop tasks: 0/50 versus 1/50 successes; 96/100 episodes reached the 10-round cap.

Scope. Downstream benefit and equivalence remain unestablished; one replay state and limited trajectory observability restrict interpretation.

Full study description — ExpeL

Mechanism-Level Audit of ExpeL

I audited ExpeL as a follow-up to Memory-Collapse-Eval. In a deterministic shared-state EDIT replay, resetting inherited support moved the edited rule from rank 2 to rank 15 while preserving all 36 rule texts.

A controlled 50-task paired WebShop evaluation yielded 0/50 successes with inherited counts versus 1/50 with reset counts. This does not establish a reliable downstream benefit or equivalence between the policies.

One selected replay state and one rollout per memory/task limit generalization. Of 100 episodes, 96 reached the 10-round cap; trajectory observability also limited interpretation. Support counts are heuristics, not calibrated confidence.
